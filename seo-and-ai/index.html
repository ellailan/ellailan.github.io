Step 1 of 7

Expand the seed keyword using Google's SEO-specific APIs.

Why

You can't write content for keywords you don't know exist. A single seed like "protein powder" hides 500+ related search queries.

How
Call the Google Ads Keyword Planner API: generate_keyword_ideas
Send in one seed keyword as input
Google returns real queries people search, each with volume, CPC, and competition
500+ keyword ideas Search volume CPC Competition level
Step 2 of 7

Use autosuggest to find longer, related searches.

Why

Autosuggest reflects real-time popular queries, but there are long-tail variants that historical Keyword Planner data can miss, especially question-style searches.

How
Hit Google's public autocomplete endpoint directly, no key required
Send the seed, then seed+"a" through seed+"z", creating 26 requests
~10 suggestions per request, deduplicated against the Ads results
300+ long-tail variants "protein powder before or after workout" "protein powder for baking"

Step 4 of 7

Is this topic trending up, or trending down?

Why

We want to write for topics with increasing traffic and interest, not ones that are fading or seasonal, like "pumpkin protein powder" every October, need to publish at the right time.

How
pytrends pulls monthly search interest on a 0–100 scale
Seasonal if variation across the year tops 30%
Rising if the last third of the data beats the first third by 15%+
Step 5 of 7

Classify the intent of every search.

Why

Not every keyword deserves the same content. A "how to" query wants a guide; a "buy" query wants a product page.

How
Match each keyword against word patterns
"buy," "price" → transactional · "best," "vs" → commercial · "how to" → informational
Most specific pattern wins; no match defaults to informational
Informational Commercial Transactional Navigational
Step 6 of 7 · Clustering: TF–IDF

TF–IDF turns keywords into numbers.

What it is

Short for Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency. It scores every word by how distinctive it is: common in this keyword, rare across the whole list.

TF–IDF=TF×IDF
IDF=logNDF
TF how often the word appears in this keywordDF how many keywords contain the wordN total number of keywords
What comes out → the TF–IDF matrix, N = 100 keywords
wheyproteinbestshoes…
best whey protein2.30.90.50…
whey protein powder2.30.900…
best running shoes000.51.9…
How one score is derived
IDF(whey)=log10010≈2.3
IDF(best)=log10060≈0.5

whey is in 10 of 100 keywords, best in 60. Each word appears once per keyword, so TF = 1. Rows 1 and 2 look alike, so they’ll land in the same group.

Step 6 of 7 · Clustering: K-Means

K-Means groups similar keywords.

How it works

Picture every keyword as a dot on a map, placed by how similar its words are to others.

1Plot every keyword as a dot.
2Drop k random starting centers onto the map.
3Each dot joins whichever center is closest — that's its cluster.
4Slide each center to the average of its group, then repeat until nothing moves.
✓Result: your "protein powder" seed keyword now splits into clean clusters — whey v. vegan protein powder, protein bars, protein pancake recipes, weightlifting.
Step 7 of 7

Score every keyword, rank every cluster.

Every keyword gets an Opportunity Score from 0–100, built from five weighted signals:

Log-normalized and scaled to 0–100 per keyword. Clusters rank by averaging their top five keyword scores. This way, the topics with the most opportunity surface first.

The ranked clusters are packaged into a single brief, ready for an AI agent to draft each blog post directly from it.

The limits

What we don't know yet.

Does more content bring more traffic?

We think doubling output from 4 to 8 blogs a month will lift traffic to the portal, but we aren't sure if that relationship exists or if its linear.

A new type of traffic

There's new interest in GEO (generative engine optimization). GEO blogs focus on long question-answer content rather than short, commonly searched keywords.

Do clicks become customers?

This project makes a case for the use of AI in top-of-funnel marketing, but we don't know its impact on conversion or where AI fits in the rest of the funnel.
